# Reliable terminal coverage and resumable audit execution

## Decision

This is a contract-first, breaking refactor of the audit-result and evaluation-trial boundary. Legacy report, checkpoint, plan, and evaluation artifacts are rejected rather than translated. The purpose is to make the workflow truthful and restartable without changing the language-neutral security judgment boundary.

## Requirements

| ID | Requirement | Owner | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| REL-001 | An enabled vector is `completed` only when every approved obligation has a terminal closure and no phase is incomplete, failed, cancelled, or not reached. | `audit-execution/coverage-closure` | A verifier-incomplete or grounding-interrupted obligation makes vector coverage incomplete. |
| REL-002 | A terminal vector or evaluation trial preserves all earlier reached phase observations, map/posture counts, funnels, and closure state. | `audit-execution`, `evaluation` | An evidence-map, posture, investigation, grounding, verification, evaluator-persistence, or catch-path failure cannot replace earlier data with zeroes, `null`, or another phase's observation. A local continuation failure after a completed model stage is `audit-continuation-failed`, never a provider failure. |
| REL-003 | A phase or candidate-aware work checkpoint is reusable only at its exact phase boundary and resumes from the narrowest unfinished unit. | `audit-execution/checkpoints`, `evaluation` | A downstream verifier/countercheck retry never repeats matching completed map, posture, discovery, or grounding work; incomplete/null grounding instead restarts at its own earliest unfinished boundary. |
| REL-004 | A required source-inspection stage that remains uninspected after its normal retry is explicitly incomplete and retryable from its predecessor checkpoint. | `review-workflow/stages`, `audit-execution` | No tool-skipped source-deciding stage is treated as clean or silently retried from scratch. |
| REL-005 | Audit and evaluation persist one source-free phase-status projection for every vector/trial. | `audit-execution`, `evaluation` | A report can distinguish completed, incomplete, failed, cancelled, excluded-by-plan, not-applicable, and review-required work without source/model content. |
| REL-006 | One terminal reducer is the sole owner of report status, counters, CLI exit code, manifest outcome, and evaluation status. | `audit-execution/terminal-classification` | No incomplete, failed, cancelled, or not-reached enabled obligation can yield complete/exit 0 or 1. |
| REL-007 | Product runs have one exclusive owner and an atomic attempt record. | `artifact-store` | Concurrent writers cannot share a run; an interrupted attempt retains reached observations and exactly one terminal outcome. |
| REL-008 | Candidate-aware verifier and evaluation-only countercheck dispatches use one audit-run-owned transport pool and durable work records. | `audit-execution/candidate-aware-dispatch`, `audit-execution/checkpoints` | The configured vector concurrency is also the maximum number of simultaneous candidate-aware provider calls across all vectors; every work item records `pending`, `running`, or `completed`. On cancellation, queued work is cancelled, no later dispatch begins, and finalization waits for already-dispatched siblings to settle and retain their content-free observations. No candidate-count cap or evidence reduction is introduced. |
| REL-009 | Every retryable semantic work unit has an exact binding and append-only, source-free progress ledger. | `review-workflow/runtime`, `audit-execution/checkpoints` | A retry records its normalized outcome and durable progress. A repeated complete validation descriptor under the unchanged protocol and scope ends explicitly incomplete as `validation-repair-no-progress` instead of replaying indefinitely or advertising generic retryability. |
| REL-010 | A retry of schema-invalid model output receives only a sanitized correction descriptor. | `review-workflow/runtime`, `review-workflow/agents`, `model-operations` | The next same-scope attempt receives one stable descriptor made from the complete canonical set of static schema-path labels: validate labels, deduplicate, sort, then digest; never truncate the set. Distinct descriptors are never treated as identical. On a repeated descriptor, an opt-in evaluator-private diagnostic may retain that same descriptor; neither path retains model text, rejected values, source, prompts, tool payloads, or validation prose. |
| REL-011 | Candidate-grounding recovery persists one canonical outcome for every discovery seed. | `audit-execution/candidate-grounding`, `audit-execution/checkpoints` | Explicit unfinished recovery reuses grounded outcomes and reschedules only null or binding-rejected outcomes while their map/posture binding is unchanged. |
| REL-012 | A downstream stage may request candidate-blind neutral evidence-map repair through a closed gap protocol. | `audit-execution/evidence-map`, `review-workflow/stages` | Map repair receives only approved vector/map/obligations, generic gap kinds, scoped context, and scoped read-only tools; it never receives a candidate, finding, priority, expected location, label, answer key, or language rule. |
| REL-013 | Recovery leaf persistence distinguishes partial child output from terminal reduced output. | `review-workflow/runtime`, `audit-execution/checkpoints` | A child is validated only against its own exact scope; full obligation/control closure occurs once after lossless reassembly, and a partial leaf can never be reused as a terminal phase artifact. |
| REL-014 | Every reusable provider-backed semantic checkpoint carries one required content-free model observation. | `model-operations`, `audit-execution/checkpoints`, `evaluation` | Missing observations reject reuse; no-provider deterministic artifacts use an explicit execution-kind variant and do not make provider cost appear as zero. |
| REL-015 | A checkpoint-write failure is a distinct terminal operational outcome. | `audit-execution`, `evaluation`, `artifact-store` | It is recorded as `checkpoint-persistence-failed`, retains every stage reached before the write, never becomes a provider failure, and requires explicit recovery after storage is repaired. |

## Canonical phase ledger

`audit-execution` owns one append-only per-vector phase ledger. It is the only input to terminal-result construction. Its entries are ordered by the fixed lifecycle:

1. evidence mapping;
2. source posture;
3. investigation;
4. candidate grounding;
5. verification for each grounded hypothesis;
6. optional evaluation-only countercheck.

Each phase entry has one of `not-reached`, `reused`, `completed`, `incomplete`, `failed`, or `cancelled`, plus its own content-free observation when a provider request was attempted. A phase may add validated redacted artifacts only through its existing feature-owned schema. A provider-backed phase is `completed` only after its model output has been converted to that phase's canonical result, including deterministic source-reference, obligation, predecessor, and checkpoint projections that could otherwise stop successor work. This conversion is inside the common scoped lifecycle, before its completed observation or durable artifact. A projection failure retains the active stage's complete request/tool/cost observation and ends it as `failed` with a stable content-free code; it cannot surface later as a generic vector failure or erase spent work. A static model-output validation failure may retry only inside the same exact scope with a stable descriptor built from the complete canonical set of static schema-path labels: validated, deduplicated, sorted, and never truncated before its digest is calculated. It stops as terminal `validation-repair-no-progress` when that exact descriptor repeats under the unchanged protocol and scope, when an existing terminal provider condition applies, or when lossless evidence preservation is impossible. The terminal state is visibly incomplete and is not generically retryable. A fresh explicit run may attempt the work again only with a changed identity, such as changed protocol or scope; it does not reuse the terminal state. The terminal repeat retains the same descriptor solely for the opt-in evaluator-private diagnostic; product records retain only the stable terminal code and source-free observations. Neither form contains rejected values, validation messages, source/context, prompts, tool payloads, raw model output, credentials, answer keys, or evaluator labels. A reducible scoped phase additionally owns one exact-binding, source-free context-overflow topology ledger. It records only deterministic scope hashes, parent/child identities, normalized overflow transitions, stable terminal codes, timestamps, and an execution union on every terminal child transition: `{ kind: provider, modelObservation }` for any provider attempt, or `{ kind: deterministic }` only for an owned no-dispatch empty child. It never caches a child model output. `evidence-mapping`, candidate-blind `evidence-map-repair`, `source-posture`, and canonical `candidate-grounding` own validated redacted leaf artifacts: the atomic writer persists a schema-v3 marked `partial` child artifact before its matching `completed` topology transition, and restart reuses it only when the immutable binding, phase, opaque phase-input fingerprint, recovery protocol, root scope, child identity, child scope hash, and exact execution union all match. The phase-input fingerprint binds the complete source-free semantic predecessor state: a repaired map invalidates posture and later work; a recomputed posture invalidates grounding and later work. A mismatched predecessor never reuses an old child. Repair leaves persist only source-validated append-only neutral facts and have phase-named artifact paths, so they cannot collide with initial map leaves. A recovered posture or grounding leaf is rejected before persistence unless every retained map fact, posture assessment, seed outcome, and projected source evidence belongs to that exact child scope. A map leaf validates neutral facts and local source provenance without requiring vector-wide obligation/control representation. A posture leaf validates each assessment only against map facts and controls available in that child scope. The phase reducer performs full obligation/control closure once after all exact children are reassembled and publishes the separately marked terminal artifact. Other phases must re-run a completed child rather than retain raw output.

Candidate-aware work is transport-scheduled, not evidence-limited. The audit creates one FIFO pool per run with capacity equal to the validated `maxParallelVectors` setting. Every verifier and optional evaluation-only countercheck enters that same pool, including work from concurrently executing vectors. The pool preserves input/result order and terminal outcomes; it has no candidate, result, source, context, or tool-call ceiling. Before dispatch, each exact candidate phase writes `pending`, then `running`, then a redacted `completed` result. Its identity binds the sealed run/vector, provider/model, verifier route, complete workflow protocol, candidate-grounding protocol, phase, ordinal, and opaque digest of the exact canonical candidate input. Its checkpoint also owns any source-free context-overflow topology for that same candidate phase; the topology binds the recovery protocol and root scope, while the enclosing checkpoint supplies the candidate/route/phase binding. A verifier or countercheck has no lossless child-decision reducer: after a normalized root overflow it records that topology and returns explicit incomplete coverage without dispatching child splits. An explicit retry with the identical topology performs no further provider call; a changed provider, route, candidate, protocol, or scope invalidates the checkpoint and begins a new attempt. Parsed model rationale is never checkpointed.

The finalizer derives coverage, errors, observations, admission funnels, and closure matrix from this ledger. It must not receive positional observation arguments or construct a second phase summary. A catch path finalizes the ledger it has, records the stable failure code, and leaves unreached work visible. If a required draft, recovery, candidate-aware, or terminal-vector checkpoint write fails, the outcome is `checkpoint-persistence-failed`, not an artifact or provider failure. The finalizer retains all stages reached before that write, suppresses unpersisted findings from successful output, and does not dispatch a replacement provider call. Recovery is explicit and begins only after the storage failure has been corrected.

Before opening recovery state, a run obtains an exclusive output-root lease. It writes an atomic content-free attempt record after root/configuration identity validation and transitions it from `starting` to exactly one terminal outcome in `finally`. Before its first public report write, it durably records a private `prepared` publication intent containing the exact report id and canonical digest while retaining the immutable snapshot. Only after every public JSON, Markdown, and manifest write completes does it transition that binding to `published` with a terminal audit outcome. A crash or publication failure after preparation leaves the private binding and snapshot intact; explicit resume regenerates and publishes from the exact retained audit state rather than trusting a partial public artifact. Snapshot cleanup is a separate closed state (`release-pending`, `released`, or `release-failed`) and cannot rewrite a completed audit outcome or invalidate an already published report. A pre-existing lease fails closed: portable PID-file locking has no race-free stale-owner takeover, so the application never deletes a lease based on process or time inference. An operator may resolve a known-abandoned lease outside a running reviewer process and then use normal explicit resume against the retained immutable snapshot. Artifact reads never create directories. Artifact writes create only containment-proven missing components, use a temporary sibling plus atomic rename, and revalidate containment immediately before rename.

## Obligation closure

For each approved obligation, closure is exactly one of:

| Closure | Meaning | Vector completion effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `no-source-backed-candidate` | Discovery completed and declared no valid seed for the obligation. This is not a deterministic safety conclusion. | terminal |
| `candidate-rejected` | A map/posture-bound candidate completed verifier rejection for the obligation. | terminal |
| `candidate-admitted` | A candidate completed verification and report admission. | terminal |
| `review-required` | A structurally valid candidate conflicted with candidate-blind posture and entered the human queue. | terminal but separately visible |
| `incomplete` | Required map, posture, discovery, grounding, verification, or reconciliation did not complete. | non-terminal |
| `not-reached` | A predecessor stopped before this obligation could be closed. | non-terminal |
| `not-applicable` | The workflow completed a crisp source- or separately labelled context-backed reason that the exact obligation does not apply. | terminal and neutral |

`candidate-not-admitted` is removed because it conflates terminal rejection with incomplete verification. A candidate's verifier disposition determines the closure; deterministic code may only check declared state, identity, scope, and schema integrity.

Disabled vectors are `excluded-by-plan`, visible, neutral, and outside the denominator. Enabled vectors with no matched admitted paths are not automatically clean or not-applicable. A complete run requires a terminal closure for every enabled obligation and no reached phase with `incomplete`, `failed`, or `cancelled`. If a valid partial report exists and any enabled obligation is incomplete, failed, cancelled, or not-reached, the result is partial/exit 3. Only complete runs reach exit 0 (no confirmed findings) or 1 (one or more confirmed findings). Invalid/safety/contract input is exit 2. Provider or infrastructure failure that prevents a valid report is exit 4.

## Checkpoint and retry protocol

Product checkpoints remain beneath the configured separate output root. Evaluation uses a separate ignored evaluator work root with the same strict checkpoint schemas and atomic writer; its published run JSON and Markdown remain source-free and never contain checkpoint artifacts.

On retry or resume, the evaluator first re-inventories the selected variant, then reuses a matching generated-plan draft only when its run configuration, trial id, target fingerprint, and context digest match exactly. It starts audit with that same approved-plan projection. The resume loader validates every exact candidate-aware checkpoint—including `pending`, `running`, and completed incomplete records—rejects duplicate identities, and constructs one opaque `AuditResumeState`; the audit core cannot receive or search independent raw artifact arrays. One feature-owned, pure recovery resolver selects the smallest exact incomplete boundary from that state. A compatible canonical grounding draft is independently sufficient to preserve its map/posture/discovery predecessors, obligation closures, complete source-free funnels, grounding observation, and every durable grounded candidate; it is reusable even when no verifier or countercheck checkpoint was ever written. The normal candidate-aware lifecycle then creates/reschedules its first `pending` unit and preserves its `pending` → `running` → `completed` ownership. A compatible downstream checkpoint narrows recovery further to that exact unit. Only a seed outcome explicitly defined as unfinished (`binding-rejected` or a valid `map-insufficient` repair request) may restart from its own earliest candidate-blind boundary; `no-source-backed-candidate` remains a declared closure and `grounded` never redispatches discovery or grounding. An incomplete map/posture output is never checkpointed as a reusable predecessor. A changed run id, plan/configuration identity, target fingerprint, provider/model, verifier route, complete workflow protocol, candidate-grounding protocol, vector id, candidate identity, or phase rejects reuse before target/provider work. On explicit resume, an exact persisted normalized-overflow topology may skip only the already-rejected parent and reconstruct the same children from the retained immutable snapshot. A changed recovery protocol or reconstructed scope hash rejects the topology before provider dispatch. Completed evidence-mapping, source-posture, and canonical-grounding children are reusable only when their matching validated leaf artifact exists; every other completed child is re-run until that phase gains its own validated leaf contract. A topology event alone never makes child output reusable.

## Progress-aware semantic recovery

The shared stage coordinator owns an append-only attempt ledger for each exact work-unit binding. Each ledger row contains only the stage/work-unit identity, protocol/route/input fingerprints, ordinal, closed failure or progress signature, source-inspection completion, durable-progress flag, content-free observation, and terminal transition. It never contains a model response, validation message or value, source/context content, tool payload, candidate statement, or answer-key data. There is no product maximum for attempts, tools, files, facts, candidates, obligations, bytes, or total work. Recovery stops only after terminal success, normalized cancellation, non-retryable provider failure, a repeated no-progress signature, a contradictory reduction, or inability to preserve all evidence.

The coordinator routes normalized context overflow only to the existing lossless range/context recovery protocol; timeout/cancellation to terminal `provider-cancelled`; provider-declared retriable failures to the provider retry policy; and a completion without required source inspection to an unchanged-scope inspection repair. For strict output validation it derives a sanitized stable descriptor from the complete canonical set of static schema-path labels only: validate, deduplicate, sort, then digest without truncation. Distinct canonical descriptors must never be treated as identical. The descriptor is supplied to the next exact-scope prompt. A repeated descriptor, including an alternating sequence that revisits an earlier descriptor, is terminal `validation-repair-no-progress`; its repeated descriptor is available only to an opt-in evaluator-private diagnostic. It is visibly incomplete coverage, never clean, and not generically retryable. A fresh explicit run may attempt again only when its bound workflow identity has changed; it must not reuse the terminal result. Raw invalid output is never included in a correction.

Candidate-grounding drafts retain exactly one canonical outcome per valid discovery seed: `grounded`, `no-source-backed-candidate`, `map-insufficient`, or `binding-rejected`. `map-insufficient` may exist only with a declared generic map-gap request bound to that seed's exact approved obligations; it triggers candidate-blind map repair rather than being treated as a terminal clean absence. The resolver retains every grounded outcome and all other durable draft data before deciding whether a seed is explicitly retryable. It may schedule only the exact retryable seed work against the same map/posture binding and then use the canonical merge/projection path; it must never replay discovery or already-completed grounding merely because a later candidate-aware checkpoint is absent. A changed map or posture invalidates dependent seed work from its earliest candidate-blind predecessor. Existing observations are registered once before any new dispatch.

A grounding or verifier boundary may return a source-free `map-insufficient` request bound to exact approved obligation IDs and one or more closed generic needs: `operation-evidence-missing`, `unsafe-condition-relation-missing`, `control-coverage-missing`, or `source-relation-unresolved`. This opens a candidate-blind mapper repair that receives only the approved vector and obligations, current validated map, gap kinds, unchanged scoped manifest/context, and read-only tools. It cannot receive candidates, findings, priorities, expected locations, evaluation labels, answer keys, paired variants, or language/parser/static-rule data. A repair may append validated neutral facts or explicitly add none. The map draft checkpoints its input/output map fingerprints, append count, and content-free repair observation before dependent work restarts. A changed canonical map fingerprint invalidates posture and all later dependent work; a posture draft and grounding draft carry their exact predecessor fingerprints and are therefore automatically recomputed when stale. An unchanged map combined with the same gap signature becomes explicit incomplete coverage.

Every reusable audit-stage decision declares the same required closed `execution` union: `kind: provider` requires exactly one content-free `modelObservation`; `kind: deterministic` has no observation. This includes evidence-map drafts, source-posture drafts, each evidence-map repair attempt, candidate-aware verifier/countercheck terminal result, recovery leaf, and terminal topology event. Missing, top-level, or mismatched telemetry rejects reuse before dispatch. A deterministic variant is valid only for an owned no-dispatch decision. This preserves exact cost reconstruction without treating unavailable cost as zero.

Before dispatching resumed audit work, the shared observed-cost guard registers every matching generated-plan and audit-checkpoint content-free stage observation, including incomplete, failed, and cancelled terminal vectors that are excluded from execution reuse during explicit unfinished recovery. It adds an exact observation once even when a successor, terminal vector, or enclosing trial checkpoint also retained it. Thus a process stop between any checkpoint write and its enclosing trial artifact cannot make already-observed cost disappear from a configured ceiling. The one-case provider smoke follows this same checkpoint-derived accounting path before it may dispatch unfinished work.

An evaluator trial may be resumed only while its configuration fingerprint and case-variant target fingerprint match. A previous `completed` trial is immutable and reusable. Previous `incomplete`, `failed`, or `cancelled` trials are reusable only as phase predecessors when retry is requested; they are never reused as a completed score. If a planning call or audit report was reached before a later evaluator failure, the failed trial retains those exact source-free stage observations and any returned source-free audit projection; it never replaces reached usage/cost telemetry with `null` merely because the score is ineligible. A completed stage followed by a checkpoint-write failure remains a completed retained stage; failure describes persistence, not the model response. The enclosing provider-evaluation command checkpoint independently closes as `failed` or `cancelled` after an orchestration exception; no known stopped command remains `running`.

A command checkpoint that completed publication but retained unfinished trials is recoverable only through the same explicit retry flag. Its previous public terminal directory is revalidated and atomically retained below the private evaluator work root before the checkpoint re-enters audit work. Completed trials remain reused; only unfinished trials can dispatch. An ordinary completed run cannot be reopened.

## Required-inspection semantics

Evidence mapping and candidate-blind posture already require source inspection for a non-empty manifest. Investigation, grounding, and verification must declare in their feature contract whether source inspection is required for their returned disposition. An inspection is satisfied only by a completed scoped `repo_read` or `repo_grep` call that returned a result to the model. An attempted, rejected, or `repo_list` call never satisfies it. A required stage may return `incomplete` after the same-scope normal retry; it cannot become a clean absence/rejection solely because no successful source tool result was available. This is a protocol state rule, not a parser, regex, language hint, benchmark, or security detector.

Harness-normalized timeout and cancellation categories are terminal provider-neutral stops. The shared stage boundary maps either to the stable `provider-cancelled` code without reading provider text and does not apply the normal fresh retry. Product and provider-evaluation deadlines default to disabled (`0`); a non-zero operator-selected deadline is an explicit operational stop, never a source, context, tool, or result cap. Candidate-aware cancellation closes the shared dispatch pool so queued work cannot spend more provider calls. Audit derives `cancelled` vector coverage from that code; evaluation derives a `cancelled` trial when any returned vector is cancelled or when planning stops that way before audit. Explicit unfinished recovery is the only path that may reattempt it.

The shared stage boundary preserves only a closed, provider-neutral model-failure token: `provider-network`, `provider-rate-limited`, `provider-unavailable`, `provider-http-error`, or `provider-response-invalid`; an absent or unrecognized normalized reason remains `provider-failure`. A failure in deterministic audit continuation after a completed stage is the distinct closed `audit-continuation-failed` token. It is not provider-retryable and must retain the completed stage observation plus every earlier validated map/posture projection. Runtime normalization and evaluator-private diagnostics use the same cycle-safe cause-and-aggregate traversal, so wrapped provider failures cannot receive contradictory classifications. Provider messages, request identifiers, status bodies, headers, parameters, and source/model content are never copied into artifacts, logs, reports, or an error code. For a Harness-normalized model failure, the normal fresh retry follows the Harness retryability declaration and never redispatches a harness-declared non-retryable failure. Other transient internal errors retain the existing one same-scope retry. The report's retryable flag uses that same closed application error vocabulary; it is not inferred from provider text.

The versioned Bun patch for the installed Purista Harness removes its undocumented 64-step clamp and preserves the caller's configured agent-loop limit. Product harness configuration uses no fixed loop cap or default deadline. An operator-selected non-zero deadline or cancellation remains an explicit operational stop. If a future explicit finite Harness limit is reached, the shared boundary preserves the distinct `agent-loop-budget-exceeded` code. It is never rewritten as a provider failure or clean completion, and the affected work remains incomplete until explicit unfinished recovery.

Overflow reduction may deduplicate only byte-identical stage artifacts with the same declared identity. Conflicting evidence-map facts, mixed null/non-null groundings for one seed, or conflicting `not-applicable` closure results make the reduced stage incomplete; a reducer must not choose one partition's candidate, neutral result, or fact over another's. This conservative rule preserves ambiguity for explicit recovery instead of converting it into a source-backed conclusion.

Product contracts impose no fixed total cardinality ceiling on approved plan vectors, obligations, scope globs, source evidence, map facts, posture assessments, discovery seeds, grounded candidates, verifier reconciliations, findings, review-required items, or audit errors. A transport/work-unit implementation may bound one I/O transaction, but it must continue exact work until all eligible items are represented. If a provider cannot produce a valid complete response, the stage is incomplete and uses the existing provider-signalled recovery path; it may not truncate or discard items to satisfy a schema limit.

## Data protection and operations

The phase ledger and evaluator work root are sensitive local operational data. They use the existing output jail, atomic writes, exact binding checks, redaction, and no-content telemetry. Logs and public reports may expose only identifiers, closed outcome tokens, counts, hashes, timings, token/cost observations, and stable redacted error codes. Operators resume with the normal explicit retry/resume command; they do not edit checkpoints.

## Verification

- Unit and contract tests cover every ledger transition, all early and catch-path finalizers, closure conservation, and legacy-artifact rejection.
- Integration tests prove phase reuse starts at the smallest exact unfinished boundary, including a crash after a valid grounding draft but before the first candidate-aware checkpoint, and does not repeat an observed completed request.
- Fake-provider tests prove a repeated uninspected completion, verifier `incomplete`, cancellation, and provider failure remain incomplete rather than completed.
- Privacy tests prove evaluator reports and telemetry contain no source, prompts, tools, raw output, answer keys, or work-root contents.
